As 2025 draws to a close, Taiwan has experienced at least three waves of large-scale phishing emails, targeting NGO workers, academics, and people engaged in democracy and human-rights work. These incidents were publicly confirmed by AIT Taiwan, reported by RightPlus, and widely discussed within civic networks.

The attackers impersonated colleagues and used “conference invitations” to lure victims into clicking harmful links. Despite visible red flags — simplified Chinese wording, incorrect event details, unfamiliar email domains — the scale of distribution suggests organized malicious intent.ns there, people might shocked but take it serious but not too serious, yet overseeing these three times, there are some insights I got from my own observation:
 
---
## Academia: Taiwan’s Hidden Vulnerable Sector

NGOs have steadily improved digital security because:

- Data leak incidents forced attention

- Mandarin security guides are increasing

- Peer networks actively share alerts

- Civil-society workers have learned awareness through experience.

But academia faces systemic barriers:

| Challenge                           | Result                             |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Security isolated in IT departments | Slow response capability           |
| Rigid admin procedures              | Systems remain outdated            |
| No required cyber training          | Risk depends on personal knowledge |
| No culture of risk-sharing          | Threats stay invisible             |

"Knowledge producers should never be left to fend for themselves against digital threats."
---

## Attack — or Digital Reconnaissance?

Across the most recent wave, I identified at least four institutions affected.
Though recipients came from different sectors, they share one common factor:

➡ Participation in democracy, social movements, public advocacy

Even a failed phishing attempt offers valuable intelligence for attackers:

- Who opens what

- Which systems and networks are used

- How people are connected

- Weak points in communication chains

What if... these "Repeated Attempts" = <b>improving the next attack</b>?!

Some expert investigation is ongoing — details cannot yet be disclosed.

## New Work Norm: Verify Before You Trust

Past:

“Is this event worth attending?”

Now:

“Is this event even real?”
“Can anyone I trust confirm?”

This new normal affects:

- Junior onboarding and mentorship

- Event coordination and collaboration

- How networks validate information

Digital safety is now part of professional literacy.

Shared Responsibility: Universities Must Step Up

Digital security should be developed across:

| Level                               | RWhat needs to happen                           |
| ----------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| SUniversities                       | Mandatory cyber hygiene training; system updates|
| Departments	                        | Create risk-sharing communication channels      |
| Government                          | Provide helplines, toolkits, funding, guidance  |
| Civil Society                       | Share practical knowledge and alerts            |
